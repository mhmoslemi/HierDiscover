The evaluation function is  

\[
\mathrm{score}= \frac{2\;n\;\max(\,a*a\,)}{(\sum a)^2},
\]

where `a*a` is the (full) self‑convolution of the non‑negative vector `a`
and `n = len(a)`.  
Because the score is *scale invariant* we may insist on `∑a = 1`; then  

\[
\mathrm{score}= 2\,n\,\max(a*a).
\]

For a *uniform* vector (`a_i = 1/n`) we have `max(a*a)=1/n` and the score
equals `2`.  The theoretical lower bound is `1` (the average of the
convolution is `1/(2n‑1)`).  The construction below reaches the value
`1.5`, which is already below the required `1.503`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Analytic construction

Take a length `n` that is a multiple of three and put the total mass
`1` into two equal blocks of length `L = n/3` placed at the two ends of
the vector:

```
a_i = 0.5/L   for i in [0, L)   or   i in [n‑L, n)
a_i = 0       otherwise
```

*Sum*: each block contributes `L·(0.5/L)=0.5`, therefore `∑a = 1`.

*Self‑convolution*: inside each block the number of overlapping pairs
at the centre is exactly `L`.  The corresponding value is  

\[
\max(a*a) = L\Bigl(\frac{0.5}{L}\Bigr)^2 = \frac{0.25}{L}.
\]

Hence  

\[
\text{score}=2\,n\,\frac{0.25}{L}= \frac{n}{2L}= \frac{n}{2\,(n/3)}=1.5 .
\]

For any `n` that is *not* a multiple of three we obtain a score slightly
above `1.5`; choosing `n = 6000 = 3·2000` gives the exact value `1.5`.
