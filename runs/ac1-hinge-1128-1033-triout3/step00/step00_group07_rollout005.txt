The evaluation function is  

\[
E(a)=\frac{2\,n\;\max\limits_{k}(a*a)_k}{\bigl(\sum_i a_i\bigr)^2},
\]

where `a*a` is the discrete (full) convolution of the non‑negative vector `a`
with itself and `n = len(a)`.

For any non‑negative vector we have

* the central entry of the convolution equals \(\sum_i a_i^2\);
* by Cauchy–Schwarz
  \[
  \sum_i a_i^2 \ge \frac{\bigl(\sum_i a_i\bigr)^2}{n},
  \]
  with equality **iff** all entries are identical.

Therefore

\[
E(a)=\frac{2n\,\max_k (a*a)_k}{(\sum_i a_i)^2}
   \ge \frac{2n\,\sum_i a_i^2}{(\sum_i a_i)^2}
   \ge \frac{2n\;(\sum_i a_i)^2/n}{(\sum_i a_i)^2}=2,
\]

and the lower bound `2` is attained **exactly** by any uniform (constant)
sequence.  No other sequence can give a smaller value – the bound `2` is
theoretical optimum.

Consequently the best possible candidate is a uniform vector.  The provided
global `height_sequence_1` may already be uniform; nevertheless we construct a
fresh uniform sequence (length 1153, the length of the previously known
construction) and optionally perform a short random local‑search just to confirm
that no improvement exists.

The implementation below follows this reasoning.  It returns the uniform
