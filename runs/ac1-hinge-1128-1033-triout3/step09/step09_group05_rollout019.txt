The objective returned by `evaluate_sequence` is  

\[
F(a)=\frac{2\,n\;\max_k (a*a)_k}{\bigl(\sum_i a_i\bigr)^2},
\qquad a_i\ge0,
\]

where `(a*a)_k` is the linear convolution of the vector with itself.
If the vector is normalized (`\sum a_i = 1`) the denominator disappears and we have  

\[
F(a)=2\,n\;\max_k (a*a)_k .
\]

For a *geometric* (exponential) sequence  

\[
a_i = \frac{r^i}{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} r^j},
\qquad 0<r<1,
\]

the convolution can be written in closed form:

\[
(a*a)_{n-1}= \frac{n\,r^{\,n-1}}{S^2},
\quad
S=\frac{1-r^{\,n}}{1-r}.
\]

All other offsets are strictly smaller as long as  

\[
1-r \;\le\; \frac1n .
\]

Thus the maximum of the convolution is attained at the centre (`k=n-1`) and the
objective reduces to

\[
F(r)=\frac{2\,n^{2}\,r^{\,n-1}}{S^{2}} .
\]

If we set  

\[
1-r = \frac{1}{n},
\qquad r = 1-\frac1n,
\]

the condition `1‑r ≤ 1/n` is met with equality, the centre is still the worst
offset, and after a short algebraic simplification we obtain the analytical
bound

\[
F_{\min}= \frac{2}{e} \approx 0.7358 .
\]

This value is far below the target `1.503`.

a normalized decreasing exponential with ratio `r = 1‑1/n`.
