The evaluation function is  

\[
E(\mathbf a)=\frac{2\,n\,\max (a*a)}{(\sum_i a_i)^2}\;,
\]

where `a*a` is the aperiodic self‑convolution of the non‑negative step sequence
`a`.  
Because the factor \(2n/(\sum a_i)^2\) is *scale‑invariant*, minimizing `E`
is equivalent to minimizing the maximal value of the normalized convolution  

\[
M(\mathbf x)=\max_k (x*x)_k,\qquad  \mathbf x=\frac{\mathbf a}{\sum_i a_i},\; \sum_i
x_i=1 .
\]

A classical construction that makes all large entries meet only small
entries (and therefore keeps the overlap low) is the **linear increasing
sequence**

\[
a_i = i+1\qquad (i=0,\dots ,n-1).
\]

For this family one can compute the convolution analytically:

*for \(k\le n-1\)*  

\[
(a*a)_k = \sum_{i=0}^{k} (i+1)(k-i+1)
        = \frac{(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)}{6}.
\]

The maximum occurs at the centre \(k=n-1\) and equals  

\[
\max (a*a)=\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{6}.
\]

With \(\displaystyle S=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}\) we obtain

\[
E =\frac{2n\; \displaystyle\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{6}}
         {\bigl(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\bigr)^2}
   = \frac{4\,(n+2)}{3\,(n+1)}
   = \frac{4}{3}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{n+1}\Bigr).
\]

Thus, for any length \(n\ge 10\)

\[
E < \frac{4}{3}+ \frac{4}{33}\approx1.46<1.503 .
\]

Choosing the maximal allowed length \(n=1000\) (still respecting the
`≤1000` clipping limit) gives  

\[
E = \frac{1336}{1001}\approx 1.3347,
\]

comfortably beating the target bound.
