The evaluation function is  

\[
\text{score}= \frac{2\,n\;\max (a*a)}{\bigl(\sum a\bigr)^2},
\]

which is *scale‑invariant*: multiplying every entry by a constant does **not**
change the score.  
For a sequence of the form  

\[
a_i = (i+1)^p ,\qquad i=0,\dots ,n-1 ,
\]

the convolution at the centre is  

\[
\max (a*a)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(i+1)^p\,(n-i)^p
          = n^{2p+1}\,B(p+1,p+1)+O(n^{2p}),
\]

where \(B\) is the Beta‑function.  
Using the known identities  

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n} i^p \;=\; \Theta\!\bigl(n^{p+1}\bigr),\qquad
B(p+1,p+1)=\frac{(p!)^2}{(2p+1)!},
\]

the score simplifies (the dependence on the size \(n\) cancels) to  

\[
\text{score}=2\,(p+1)^2\,B(p+1,p+1).
\]

* For \(p=0\) (uniform heights) we obtain 2.  
* For \(p=1\) (linear growth) we obtain \(\tfrac43\approx1.33\).  
* For \(p=2\) (quadratic growth) the score is \(\tfrac35=0.6\).  
* For \(p=3\) (cubic growth) the score is \(\tfrac{8}{35}\approx0.229\).

Thus a **cubic** (or any higher‑order) polynomial height profile already
beats the target 1.503 by a large margin.

`max(...,1000.0)`. Because the score is scale‑invariant we can simply
scale the whole vector so that the largest entry equals 1000; all other
