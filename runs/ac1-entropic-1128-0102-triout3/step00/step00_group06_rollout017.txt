Propose a candidate step‑function sequence.

The evaluation metric is
    E = 2 * n * max(conv(a, a)) / (sum(a) ** 2)

which is scale‑invariant: only the shape of the non‑negative vector matters.
The best known analytic shape for this problem is a one‑sided
power‑law a[i] ∝ 1/√(i+1).  With the usual normalisation (sum = √(2n))
its score equals π/2 ≈ 1.5708.  By starting from this analytically good
distribution and performing a fast local search that repeatedly lowers the
largest convolution entry we can push the score a little further – often
below the target 1.503.

The search works as follows:

* Initialise a monotone decreasing sequence a[i] = 1/√(i+1) and rescale it so
  that sum(a) = √(2 n) (the normalisation used in the original SOTA code).

* Repeatedly:
    – compute the full convolution,
    – locate the index k where the convolution is maximal,
    – among all pairs (i, j) with i + j = k find the pair with the largest
      product a[i]·a[j],
    – move a small fraction of the larger element of that pair to a location
      that presently has a very small value (chosen among the lowest 5 % of the
      entries).  The move conserves the total mass; a tiny re‑normalisation
      is applied to compensate for rounding errors.

      ever seen.

until the time budget is exhausted (≈ 990 s, leaving a small safety margin).
The algorithm is completely deterministic apart from the random choices

FFT‑based convolution), and fits comfortably inside the 1 CPU limit.
