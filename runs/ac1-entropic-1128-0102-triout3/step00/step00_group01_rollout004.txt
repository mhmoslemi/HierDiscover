The evaluation function is  

\[
E(a)=\frac{2\,n\;\max_k (a*a)_k}{(\sum_i a_i)^2},
\]

where `n = len(a)` and `a*a` is the (full) convolution of `a` with itself.  
The central entry of the convolution is always  

\[
(a*a)_{n-1}= \sum_i a_i^2 .
\]

Hence  

\[
\max_k (a*a)_k \ge \sum_i a_i^2 .
\]

For a fixed length `n` and a fixed total mass  

\[
S = \sum_i a_i,
\]

the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives  

\[
\sum_i a_i^2 \;\ge\; \frac{S^2}{n},
\]

with equality **iff** all entries are identical.  
Putting the two inequalities together we obtain the universal lower bound  

\[
E(a) \ge \frac{2\,n\;S^2/n}{S^2}=2 .
\]

Thus the best possible value of the evaluation function is `2.0`, attained by
any *uniform* non‑negative sequence (e.g. `[1,1,…,1]`).  No other shape can
beat this bound, so the optimal answer is a uniform step function.
