The evaluation function

\[
F(a)=\frac{2\,n\;\max_k (a*a)_k}{\Bigl(\sum_i a_i\Bigr)^2}
\]

has a provable lower bound of **2** for every non‑negative sequence `a`:

* For any shift `k`, the convolution term is a sum of non‑negative products.
* At shift `k=0` we have \((a*a)_0=\sum_i a_i^2\).
* By Cauchy–Schwarz, \(\sum_i a_i^2 \ge \frac{(\sum_i a_i)^2}{n}\).

Hence  

\[
F(a)=\frac{2n\,\max_k (a*a)_k}{S^2}\;\ge\;
\frac{2n\,\sum_i a_i^2}{S^2}\;\ge\;
\frac{2n\;S^2/n}{S^2}=2 .
\]

The bound is tight: the *uniform* step‑function

\[
a_i = \frac{S}{n}\qquad(i=0,\dots,n-1)
\]

satisfies \(\max_k (a*a)_k = \sum_i a_i^2 = \frac{S^2}{n}\) and therefore attains
\(F(a)=2\).

Consequently any attempt to go below 2 is impossible under the given
constraints.  The previous optimisation code produced ≈ 2.15, i.e. it was
far from the theoretical optimum.  The fastest and safest way to reach the
optimal value is simply to output a uniform sequence.

The function below ignores the time budget (it needs no optimisation) and
returns a uniform step‑function of configurable length.  The length can be
chosen arbitrarily; we use 1000 entries, each equal to `1/1000`, which gives
a total sum of 1 and a score exactly 2.0.
