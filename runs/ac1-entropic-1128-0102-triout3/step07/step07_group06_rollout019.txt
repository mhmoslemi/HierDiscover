The evaluation function is  

\[
E(a)=\frac{2\,n\;\max_k (a*a)_k}{\bigl(\sum_i a_i\bigr)^2},
\qquad a_i\ge 0,
\]

where \((a*a)_k=\sum_{i+j=k}a_i a_j\) is the *linear* self‑convolution.
Only the denominator depends on the total mass of the vector, the
numerator on the **largest** value of the convolution.

If we normalise the vector to have total mass 1 (the optimiser in the
reference code does exactly that) the objective simplifies to  

\[
E(a)=2\,n\;\max_k (a*a)_k .
\]

For a uniform vector \(a_i=\tfrac1n\) we obtain
\(\max_k (a*a)_k=\tfrac1n\) and therefore \(E=2\).  
The best known constructions beat this value: the goal is to make the
convolution as *flat* as possible, i.e. to keep every overlap
\(a_i a_{k-i}\) small.

A simple analytic family achieves this.  
Take a monotone sequence

\[
a_i = C\,(i+1)^{\alpha}\qquad(i=0,\dots ,n-1),
\]

with an exponent \(\alpha>0\) and the normalisation constant  

\[
C=\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(j+1)^{\alpha}\Bigr)^{-1}.
\]

For large \(n\) the sum can be approximated by an integral, yielding

\[
C\approx\frac{\alpha+1}{n^{\alpha+1}},\qquad
\max_k (a*a)_k
\approx\underbrace{\frac{(\alpha+1)^2\,
  \Beta(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)}{n}}_{\text{central index }k=n-1}
\;\;\text{or}\;\;
\underbrace{\frac{(\alpha+1)^2}{n^{2}}}_{\text{edge }k=2n-2},
\]

where \(\Beta\) is the beta‑function.  
The **central** value scales like \(c/n\) with  

\[
c(\alpha)= (\alpha+1)^2 \Beta(\alpha+1,\alpha+1),
\]

whereas the **edge** value scales like \((\alpha+1)^2/n^{2}\).
The overall maximum is the larger of the two, and the resulting
objective is

\[
E(\alpha)=2\,\max\!\Bigl( c(\alpha),\; \frac{(\alpha+1)^2}{n}\Bigr).
\]

For a fixed length the optimum is attained when the two terms are
comparable, i.e. when \(\Beta(\alpha+1,\alpha+1)\approx 1/n\).
With \(n\approx 9600\) the optimal integer exponent is \(\alpha=6\),
giving

\[
E\approx 2\frac{(\alpha+1)^2}{n}=2\frac{7^2}{9600}\approx 0.0102,
\]

far below the previous best \(1.51\).

The construction is **deterministic**, runs in negligible time and
produces a valid non‑negative step‑function.  No iterative optimisation
