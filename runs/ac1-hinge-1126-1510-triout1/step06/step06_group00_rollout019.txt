"""
Simple power‑law construction for the step‑function.

The evaluation metric is
    2 * n * max(conv(a,a)) / (sum(a)**2)
and is invariant under scaling of a.
If a is normalised to sum = 1, the score reduces to
    2 * n * max(conv(a,a))

For a monotone increasing sequence a_i ∝ (i+1)^p the full
convolution attains its maximum at the centre (shift = n‑1) and
its value tends to

    max(conv) ≈ (p+1)^2 * B(p+1,p+1) / n

with B the beta‑function.
Thus the score ≈ 2 · (p+1)^2 · B(p+1,p+1) which quickly
drops when the exponent p grows.

The function below tries a few small integer exponents
(1…7), normalises the resulting vector and selects the
one with the lowest evaluated score.  The whole search takes
well under a second, leaving plenty of time for the outer
1000 s budget.
"""

    Return a step‑function (list of non‑negative floats) with a very low
    evaluation score.  The construction is a simple power‑law:
        a_i = (i+1)^p   (i = 0 … n‑1)
    normalised to sum = 1.  We test a few exponents and keep the best.
    """
    # ------------------------------------------------------------------
