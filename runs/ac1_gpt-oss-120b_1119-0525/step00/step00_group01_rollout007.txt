Optimization of the evaluation function via a discretised arcsine distribution.

The evaluation score is
    score = 2 * n * max(conv(a, a)) / (sum(a) ** 2)

Because the score is homogeneous, we may assume sum(a)=1 and minimise
    f(a) = 2 * n * max_k  Σ_i a_i a_{k-i} .
The total mass of all convolution bins is 1, therefore the theoretical
lower bound is 2n/(2n‑1) → 1 as n grows.  An exact uniform convolution is
impossible, but the continuous arcsine density
    f(x) = 1 / (π √(x (1‑x)))   on (0,1)
has the property that the sum of two independent arcsine variables is
uniform.  Discretising this density gives a sequence whose self‑convolution
is almost flat, i.e. the largest bin is close to the average 1/(2n‑1).  The
resulting score is therefore close to the optimal lower bound.

1. Choose a length n (the code prefers the supplied reference length, but

2. Build the raw arcsine weights

3. Normalise the vector to avoid pathological very‑small sums – the normalisation

The routine respects the 1000‑second overall budget, prints progress and is

def _arcsine_sequence(length: int) -> list[float]:

    total = sum(seq)
