"""
Simple constructive solution for the step‑function optimisation problem.

The evaluation metric is
    score = 2 * n * max(conv(f, f)) / (sum(f) ** 2).

Because the score is homogeneous of degree zero, scaling the whole sequence
does not change the score.  The goal is therefore to choose a *shape* for the
coefficients that makes the maximal convolution term as small as possible
relative to the ℓ₁‑norm.

A classic family of shapes with excellent properties are the monomials

    a_i = (i + 1) ** p     for i = 0 … n‑1

with an integer exponent p ≥ 1.  For this shape the dominant convolution term
occurs at the centre (index n‑1) and can be evaluated analytically:

    max(conv) = Σ_{i=1}^{n} i^p (n+1-i)^p ≈ n^{2p+1} · B(p+1, p+1),

where B is the Beta‑function.  The score then approaches

    score(p) = 2·(p+1)²·(p!)² / (2p+1)!.

For p = 1 the score is 4/3 ≈ 1.33; higher exponents give dramatically smaller
scores (e.g. p = 3 → 0.2286, p = 4 → 0.0794).  The shape is monotone, all values
are non‑negative, and by applying a uniform scaling factor we keep every entry
≤ 1000, which is the only clipping performed by `evaluate_sequence`.

The implementation below builds such a sequence (using p = 3, which already
beats the target of 1.503 by a large margin) with length taken from the
existing `height_sequence_1` if it exists, otherwise a default of 5000.
It prints the resulting score for verification and returns the list of floats.
"""
