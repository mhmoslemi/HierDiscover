The algorithm searches for a non‑negative step‑function `a = (a₀,…,a_{n‑1})` with  

\[
\sum_i a_i = \sqrt{2n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\max_k (a*a)_k
\]

as small as possible (the benchmark score equals this maximum because the
normalisation fixes the denominator).

The search consists of four stages:

1. **Very dense parametric pre‑search** – evaluate a few thousand simple
   families (`power‑law`, `exp·power‑law`, `beta‑shape`).  
   The best candidate becomes the starting point.

2. **Hill‑climbing / random jitter** – add Gaussian noise, re‑project onto the
   simplex and keep improvements.  This quickly explores a neighbourhood of
   the pre‑search optimum.

3. **Projected Adam on a soft‑max approximation of the max‑convolution** – a
   standard Adam optimiser with an exponential schedule of the soft‑max
   temperature.  The optimiser runs for a configurable slice of the remaining
   time and may be started from several jittered restarts.

4. **Aggressive deterministic mass transfer (improved)** – for the current
   maximal convolution entries we compute the exact sub‑gradient (using a
   fast FFT convolution) and move mass from the most contributing index to
   the least contributing one.  The step size is computed analytically so
   that the current peak is reduced to the second‑largest value.  The
   procedure is repeated until the time budget expires.

A final short sub‑gradient descent polishes the solution.  The best sequence
found during the whole run is returned (trailing near‑zero entries are
removed).  All operations respect the simplex constraint and the non‑negative
restriction.

competition harness provides `evaluate_sequence`.  The routine finishes well
